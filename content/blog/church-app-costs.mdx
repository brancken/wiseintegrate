Sooner or later someone asks, "Should our church have an app?" People live on their phones, and an app puts service times, events, sermons and giving one tap away. But the cost varies a great deal depending on how you go about it. Here are the three realistic options and their trade-offs.

## What are the options for a church app?

1. A custom app, built for your church by a developer.
2. A church app platform, where you pay a subscription and fill in a ready-made app.
3. A web app, sometimes called a PWA, that people install from your church's own website.

## What does a custom church app cost?

A custom app is built just for you, often as two apps: one for iPhone and one for Android. It gives you the most freedom, and it is by far the most expensive option.

You pay a developer or agency to build the app, usually a large one-off amount. Then you keep paying: apps need updates when Apple or Google change their phones and rules, and somebody has to fix problems. Prices vary so much that any single figure would mislead you. Ask two or three developers for a written quote that includes the yearly cost of looking after the app, not just building it. For most churches, a custom app is more than they need.

## What does a church app platform cost?

Several companies offer church apps on a subscription. You add your logo, sermons, events and giving, and the company publishes the app. Many also include online giving, groups or a church database.

This is cheaper and quicker than a custom app, and the company looks after the updates. The trade-off is that you pay for as long as you have the app, and giving usually has fees on each donation too. Many platforms do not publish all their prices, which depend on your church's size and the features you choose. Ask for a written quote, check what is included, and read the contract, especially what happens to your data and your app if you stop paying.

## What does it cost to put an app in the app stores?

If your app goes into the Apple App Store and Google Play, there are store fees as well:

- **Apple:** the Apple Developer Program costs 99 US dollars per membership year, and the price may vary by region. Apple says a nonprofit organisation that is officially recognised by a relevant authority, and that does not sell digital goods through its apps, can request a fee waiver. See [Apple's enrolment page](https://developer.apple.com/programs/enroll/) and [fee waiver page](https://developer.apple.com/support/fee-waiver/).
- **Google Play:** a one-time registration fee of 25 US dollars. See [Google Play's help page](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/6112435).

Both stores also review each app and have rules, including rules about selling things and taking donations inside an app. Prices and rules change, so check Apple's and Google's own websites. If you use a church app platform, ask whether these fees are included.

## What is a web app, and what does it cost?

A web app is your church website, built so that people can save it to their phone's home screen. It then opens with its own icon, like an app. There is no app store, no store fee and no review. See how it works on our own [app page](/app).

## What are the limits of a web app?

Here are the honest limits:

- It is not listed in the app stores, so people cannot find it by searching there. You need to point them to your website.
- On an iPhone, people install it from the Share button in Safari, which is a step some people do not know. A short guide on your website helps.
- A few features that native apps have may work differently or not at all in a web app.

For most churches these limits are small. People mainly want service times, events, sermons, giving details and a way to get in touch, and a web app can handle all of these well.

## Which option should your church choose?

Start with what your people actually need. If you want an app tied closely to a church database and giving, a platform can be worth the subscription. If you have very specific needs and the budget, a custom app is possible. For many churches, especially small churches and church plants, a web app from your own website gives people what they need without an ongoing app fee.
