AI is probably already in your church, whether anyone decided it should be. A volunteer drafts the newsletter with a chatbot. Someone pastes a list of names into an app to tidy it up. None of this is wrong in itself, but without clear guidance, good people can put privacy and trust at risk.

A short AI use policy helps. One or two pages agreed by your leadership can show your team where AI helps and where it does not belong.

## Why does a church need an AI policy?

There are three main reasons.

**To protect people.** Churches hold sensitive information: names, addresses, children's details, prayer requests and pastoral notes. Typing that into the wrong tool can share it with a company you know nothing about.

**To protect trust.** People need to know that when they speak to their pastor, they are speaking to a person, and that what they share stays private.

**To help your team.** Volunteers and staff often hold back from useful tools because they are unsure what is allowed. A clear policy tells them what is welcome as well as what is not.

## Where can AI help in a church?

AI can be a real help with administration, the routine work that takes hours every week. For example:

- Drafting routine emails, notices and newsletter items for a person to check
- Summarising meeting notes and listing the actions
- Tidying rosters, lists and timetables
- Turning a long policy or report into a plain-language summary
- Checking spelling and making event information easier to read

In each case a person reviews the result before it is used. People stay responsible for what goes out.

## Where does AI never belong in church life?

This is the most important part of any church AI policy. Be clear and specific:

- **Never writing or preparing sermons.** Preaching comes from prayer, study of the Scriptures and knowing your people. AI makes too many theological errors, and the pulpit is no place for them.
- **Never replacing pastoral care or counselling.** People in need deserve a real person who listens and cares.
- **Never giving spiritual guidance or praying for people.** These belong to people in relationship with God and with each other.
- **Never accessing confidential pastoral conversations.** Notes from pastoral care and counselling are never typed into an AI tool.
- **Never making theological or doctrinal decisions.** These belong to your leadership and the church's own processes.

## What else should a church AI policy cover?

**Privacy.** Say plainly what information must never go into an AI tool: personal details, children's information, prayer requests, health information and anything said in confidence. Make sure your use of AI fits your country's privacy law and your church's own privacy policy.

**Human review.** Anything AI helps to write is checked by a person before it is sent, printed or published. A named person is responsible for it, not the tool.

**An approved tools list.** List the AI tools your church has agreed to use, and who may use them. Choose tools whose privacy settings you understand, and turn off settings that let the company use your content for training where you can. Using an unlisted tool needs approval first.

**Honesty.** If AI helped substantially with something people will read, decide whether and how you will say so.

**Yearly review.** AI tools change quickly. Agree to review the policy at least once a year, and sooner if something goes wrong.

## A sample outline you can copy

Here is a simple outline. Adapt the words to your church and have your leadership approve it.

1. **Purpose.** Why we have this policy and who it applies to (staff, volunteers and leaders).
2. **Our approach.** We use AI to help with administration so our people have more time for ministry. People remain responsible for everything we do.
3. **Where AI may be used.** A short list of approved tasks, such as drafting routine emails and summarising meeting notes.
4. **Where AI is never used.** Sermons and sermon preparation, pastoral care and counselling, spiritual guidance and prayer, confidential pastoral conversations, and theological or doctrinal decisions.
5. **Privacy.** What information must never be entered into an AI tool.
6. **Human review.** Every piece of AI-assisted work is checked by a named person before it is used.
7. **Approved tools.** The tools we use and who may use them.
8. **Concerns and mistakes.** Who to talk to if something goes wrong or you are unsure.
9. **Review.** The policy is reviewed every year by the leadership.
