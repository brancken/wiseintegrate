## Why have an AI policy?

Staff and volunteers are already using AI tools, often on their own accounts, to draft notices, emails and newsletters. A short policy helps everyone know where AI can help, where it never belongs, and how to protect the people we serve.

## Who this applies to

Staff, pastors, elders, board members, and volunteers who do church administration or communications. It covers any AI tool used for church work, paid or free, including AI features inside email, office and church management software.

## Where AI never belongs

At [Your Church Name], AI will never:

1. **Write or prepare sermons.** Preaching is the work of the preacher, through prayer, study and knowing our people.
2. **Replace pastoral care or counselling.** People are cared for by people.
3. **Provide spiritual guidance or prayer** on the church's behalf.
4. **Access confidential pastoral conversations.** Pastoral and counselling conversations, notes and private prayer requests are never recorded, transcribed, summarised or stored by an AI tool.
5. **Make theological or doctrinal decisions.** These belong to [the Senior Pastor and Elders / our denomination].

## Where AI can help

AI can help with:

- administration and scheduling: agendas, rosters, bookings, reminders
- communications: first drafts of notices and routine emails
- volunteer coordination: organising sign-ups and roster requests
- newsletters and social media: layout ideas, headings, captions and proofreading
- researching resources, which a person then checks
- freeing staff and volunteers for what only people can do: being present, listening, praying and caring

## Keeping people in charge

1. **A person is responsible** for every piece of work, not the tool.
2. **Everything AI drafts is reviewed by a person** before it is sent, published or acted on.
3. **Check the facts.** AI tools can be confidently wrong. Check names, dates, Bible references, quotes and figures.
4. **Edit it into our voice**, so it reflects our beliefs and care for people.
5. **No decisions about people.** AI does not decide who serves, who receives help, or how anyone is treated.

## Protecting privacy

1. **Do not type personal or confidential pastoral information into AI tools**, unless the tool is on our approved list for that information and has been privacy-checked.
2. This includes anything about health, family matters, finances, counselling, prayer requests, safeguarding, and any information about children.
3. Never put personal information into a free or personal AI account.
4. When in doubt, leave it out, or remove names and identifying details first.
5. If personal information is entered by mistake, tell the [Privacy Officer] the same day.

New Zealand's Privacy Commissioner says the Privacy Act 2020 applies to everyone using AI tools in New Zealand, and expects organisations to check whether a tool keeps or shares what is typed into it, and to complete a privacy impact assessment before using AI with personal information. (privacy.org.nz/resources-and-learning/a-z-topics/ai/)

## Being open about AI

1. We are honest when AI has played a significant part in something we publish, for example: "Drafted with help from an AI tool and reviewed by our team."
2. We do not publish AI-generated images of real people, or images that could mislead.
3. If we ever use an automated chat tool, people will be told it is automated and how to reach a person.

## Training

Everyone who uses AI for church work reads this policy and signs to say they understand it. The [AI Lead] runs a short refresher at least once a year.

## Responsibilities

- **[Senior Pastor]:** leads our approach, approves tools, and guards the "never" list.
- **[AI Lead]:** keeps the approved tools list and runs training.
- **[Privacy Officer]:** checks privacy before tools are approved and handles any privacy incidents.
- **Every user:** follows this policy, reviews every AI draft and protects privacy.
